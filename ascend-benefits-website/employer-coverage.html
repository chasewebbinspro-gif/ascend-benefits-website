The Problem with Traditional Insurance

Why Growing Businesses Lose Every Renewal Cycle

For employers with 2–250 employees, traditional fully-insured group plans are the default — but they're rarely the best option. Here's what's actually happening to your money every year.

Premiums Rise Regardless of Your Claims

With traditional insurance, the carrier collects your premium whether your employees are healthy or not. Even if your group has a great year with minimal claims, your renewal still goes up. You never see the upside — only the downside.

Zero Transparency Into What's Driving Costs

Traditional carriers don't share your claims data. You can't see what's actually driving your costs, which makes it impossible to make smarter decisions at renewal — so you're stuck accepting whatever rate they offer.

Rising Costs Kill Your Ability to Compete for Talent

Healthcare costs that eat into your profits mean less money for salaries, growth, and competitive benefits packages. When you can't afford to offer strong benefits, top talent walks to companies that can.

Level-Funded Plans

Predictable payments + the potential to get money back
Most Popular — Best Starting Point

A level-funded plan gives your business the best of both worlds — the predictability of fixed monthly payments combined with the cost-saving potential of self-funding. Each month, you pay a set amount broken into three components: expected claims, stop-loss insurance, and administrative fees.

Unlike traditional insurance, expenses are reflected only as a percentage of actual claims — you pay for paid claims, not estimated premiums. If your employees stay healthier than projected, the surplus stays with you. At the end of the plan year, you may receive a refund on unused claims funds.

There is no cash advance required, which is typically the case with other third-party administrators — making level-funding significantly more cash-flow friendly for growing businesses. And because the plan is backed by stop-loss insurance, your maximum exposure is known and capped from day one.

Self-Funded / ERISA Plans

Maximum control, maximum savings potential
Greatest Long-Term Savings

With a self-funded plan, employee claims are paid directly from your company's budget rather than from an insurance carrier's pool. This is the strategy Fortune 500 companies have used for decades — and Ascend makes it accessible to businesses with as few as 2 employees.

Traditionally, insurance companies treat premiums as a prepayment of future claims — keeping all the profit when claims are low. Self-funding flips this model. If your employees' claims are within budget, your company keeps the surplus. You're no longer subsidizing other companies' sick employees in a pooled plan.

Of course, this model comes with risk — what if claims exceed your budget? That's where stop-loss insurance becomes essential. It acts like a high deductible, referring any claims over a predetermined limit directly to a stop-loss insurer for processing. Expenses are never charged for claims exceeding the chosen stop-loss level — giving you maximum savings potential with a hard ceiling on risk.

We work with top-rated TPAs and multiple stop-loss carriers to structure the right protection for your group, and we help you determine which benefits should be self-funded, level-funded, or fully-funded based on your specific workforce and risk tolerance.

Risk Protection

Stop-Loss Insurance: Your Safety Net

The most common concern employers have about self-funding is risk. What if an employee has a catastrophic claim? That's exactly what stop-loss insurance is designed for — and it's a core component of every self-funded and level-funded plan we structure.

Stop-loss works like a high deductible for your plan. Once a claim exceeds the predetermined stop-loss level, the stop-loss insurer takes over directly. Your company is never billed for claims above that threshold — and no fees apply to those claims either.

We partner with top-rated TPAs and multiple stop-loss carriers to find the right specific and aggregate coverage for your group — ensuring you get maximum savings potential with a hard ceiling on your worst-case exposure.

Talk to Us About Stop-Loss Coverage

Specific Stop-Loss

Protects against any single employee's claims exceeding a set threshold. Once one individual's claims cross the limit, the stop-loss carrier covers the rest — your plan never pays above that amount for any one person.

Aggregate Stop-Loss

Protects the plan as a whole. If total claims across all employees exceed the aggregate limit for the year, the stop-loss carrier absorbs the excess. Your maximum annual liability is defined from day one.

No Billing Above the Threshold

Once a claim exceeds the stop-loss level, your company is not billed — and no applicable fees are charged. The stop-loss insurer manages all claims above the limit directly.
