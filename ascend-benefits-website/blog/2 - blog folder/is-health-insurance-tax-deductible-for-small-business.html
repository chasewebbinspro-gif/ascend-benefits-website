Is Health Insurance Tax Deductible for a Small Business? A Plain-English Guide

By Chase Webb, Director of Benefit Strategies  ·  October 2026  ·  7 min read
The Short Answer

Yes. When a small business pays health insurance premiums for its employees, those payments are generally a deductible business expense, and they're tax-free to the employees. Self-employed owners, partners, and S-corp owners who own more than 2% are treated differently — they typically deduct their own premiums through the self-employed health insurance deduction instead. Small employers may also qualify for a tax credit of up to 50% of premiums paid.

Health insurance is one of the biggest line items for any small business that offers it. The good news is that the tax code is on your side. Here's how the deductions work for the business, for employees, and for you as the owner.

For the Business: Premiums Are a Deductible Expense

What your business pays toward employee health insurance premiums is generally deductible as an ordinary and necessary business expense. That applies whether you use a traditional fully insured plan, a level-funded plan, or a self-funded plan — the employer's share of the cost is a business expense either way.

For Employees: Coverage Is Tax-Free

The employer's contribution toward health coverage isn't counted as taxable income to employees. And with a Section 125 premium-only plan, employees can pay their share of premiums with pre-tax dollars, too. That lowers their taxable wages — and lowers the employer's payroll taxes on those wages as well.

That second part is worth noticing. Because pre-tax premium contributions aren't subject to FICA, a Section 125 plan creates payroll tax savings for the employer on every dollar employees contribute.

For Owners: It Depends on How Your Business Is Set Up

This is where most of the confusion comes from. The tax treatment of your own coverage depends on your business structure:

Business typeHow the owner's premiums are treated
Sole proprietor / single-member LLCNot a business expense on Schedule C. Typically deducted through the self-employed health insurance deduction on your personal return.
Partnership / multi-member LLCPremiums paid for partners are generally treated as guaranteed payments, and partners typically take the self-employed health insurance deduction.
S corporation (more than 2% owner)The S-corp pays or reimburses the premiums and reports them on the owner's W-2. The owner then typically takes the self-employed health insurance deduction.
C corporationOwner-employees are treated like other employees: the corporation deducts the premiums, and they're generally tax-free to the owner.

The rules here have real nuances — like the requirement that S-corp premiums run through payroll correctly. Your CPA should confirm the setup for your business.

The Self-Employed Health Insurance Deduction

For sole proprietors, partners, and more-than-2% S-corp owners, this deduction is the key. If you have a net profit, you can generally deduct premiums for yourself, your spouse, your dependents, and children under 27. It's taken on Schedule 1 of your Form 1040, so you don't need to itemize.

One catch: you can't take it for any month you were eligible for a subsidized employer plan — including through a spouse's job — even if you didn't enroll.

A deduction lowers your taxable income. A credit lowers your tax bill dollar-for-dollar. If your business qualifies for the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit, it can be worth far more than the deduction alone.

The Small Business Health Care Tax Credit

On top of the deduction, some small employers can claim a federal tax credit of up to 50% of the premiums they pay (35% for tax-exempt employers). To qualify, you generally need:

  • Fewer than 25 full-time equivalent employees
  • Average annual wages below an IRS limit that's adjusted each year
  • To pay at least 50% of employee-only premiums
  • Coverage purchased through the SHOP Marketplace

The credit is available for two consecutive tax years and is claimed on IRS Form 8941. Note that you can't deduct the portion of premiums you take the credit for.

Other Tax-Advantaged Options

  • HSAs. Paired with a high-deductible health plan, employer and employee contributions to a health savings account are tax-advantaged.
  • QSEHRA and ICHRA. These let employers reimburse employees tax-free for individual health insurance. A QSEHRA is capped at $6,450 self-only and $13,100 family for 2026.
  • Preventive care programs under Section 125. Some preventive care programs are structured to generate FICA savings that help fund the rest of the benefits package. Our preventive care page explains how that works.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is health insurance a deductible business expense?

Generally, yes. What a business pays toward its employees' health insurance premiums is typically deductible as an ordinary and necessary business expense, and it's tax-free to the employees.

Can a sole proprietor deduct health insurance as a business expense?

Not on Schedule C. Sole proprietors typically deduct their own health insurance premiums through the self-employed health insurance deduction on Schedule 1 of Form 1040, as long as they have a net profit and weren't eligible for a subsidized employer plan.

How do S corp owners deduct health insurance?

For owners with more than 2% of an S corporation, the S-corp pays or reimburses the premiums and includes them in the owner's W-2 wages. The owner then generally claims the self-employed health insurance deduction on their personal return.

Is there a tax credit for small businesses that offer health insurance?

Yes. The Small Business Health Care Tax Credit is worth up to 50% of premiums paid for eligible employers with fewer than 25 full-time equivalents and modest average wages who pay at least half of employee premiums and buy coverage through the SHOP Marketplace. It's available for two consecutive years.

The Bottom Line

Offering health insurance is one of the most tax-efficient ways to pay your team. The business deducts it, employees get it tax-free, and smart plan design — like Section 125 and level-funded or RBP plans — stretches those dollars further. Just make sure your CPA and your benefits consultant are talking to each other so the structure is set up right.
