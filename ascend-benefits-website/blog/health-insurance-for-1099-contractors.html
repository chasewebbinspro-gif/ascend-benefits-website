Health Insurance for 1099 Contractors and the Self-Employed: What to Know for 2026

By Chase Webb, Director of Benefit Strategies  ·  October 2026  ·  8 min read
The Short Answer

1099 contractors and self-employed people get health insurance on their own — through the ACA Marketplace (HealthCare.gov), a private individual plan, or a spouse's employer plan. If you show a profit, you can usually deduct your premiums with the self-employed health insurance deduction. For 2027 coverage, Marketplace open enrollment runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027.

When you leave a W-2 job to work for yourself, the paycheck isn't the only thing that changes. The group health plan goes with it. And if you've never shopped for your own coverage, the options can feel like a maze.

Here's how it actually works for 1099 contractors, freelancers, gig workers, and solo business owners — and what changed for 2026.

Can 1099 Contractors Get Group Health Insurance?

Usually not on their own. Group plans are built for employers with employees. A solo owner with no W-2 employees generally doesn't qualify for a traditional small group plan. That leaves three main paths: the Marketplace, private individual plans, or coverage through a spouse.

The companies you contract with also aren't required to cover you. Under the ACA, employer coverage obligations apply to employees, not independent contractors.

Option 1: The ACA Marketplace

The Marketplace (HealthCare.gov in Tennessee and most states) sells ACA-compliant plans that can't turn you down or charge more for pre-existing conditions. Depending on your income, you may qualify for a premium tax credit that lowers your monthly cost.

Important change for 2026: the enhanced premium tax credits that had been in place since 2021 expired at the start of 2026. That means:

  • Subsidies are smaller for many people who still qualify.
  • The "subsidy cliff" is back — if your household income is above 400% of the federal poverty level, you generally get no tax credit at all.
  • Full-price premiums jumped in many states. In Tennessee, the average full-price Marketplace premium rose about 37.5% for 2026.

For self-employed people whose income swings year to year, that cliff matters. Estimate your income carefully when you apply, because you'll reconcile the credit on your tax return.

Option 2: Private Individual Health Plans

Outside the Marketplace, there's a whole market of private individual plans. Some are ACA-compliant plans bought directly from a carrier. Others are medically underwritten plans that price your coverage based on your health — which can mean noticeably lower premiums for healthy people, especially those who earn too much for a subsidy.

The trade-off: underwritten plans may limit or exclude coverage for pre-existing conditions and can have different benefit rules than ACA plans. Read the plan details carefully, or work with a licensed agent who can walk you through the differences side by side.

You can compare private individual plans here.

Option 3: A Spouse's Employer Plan

If your spouse has access to an employer plan, joining it is often the simplest option. Just know that it can affect your tax deduction — more on that below.

If you earn too much for a Marketplace subsidy and you're in good health, don't assume the Marketplace is your only option. Compare it against private plans before you choose.

The Self-Employed Health Insurance Deduction

This is the part a lot of 1099 workers miss. If you have a net profit from self-employment, you can generally deduct what you pay for health insurance for yourself, your spouse, your dependents, and children under 27 — as an adjustment to income on Schedule 1 of your Form 1040. You don't have to itemize.

A few rules to know:

  • You need a profit. The deduction is tied to your self-employment earnings.
  • The month-by-month rule. You can't take the deduction for any month you were eligible to join a subsidized employer plan — including through your spouse's job — even if you didn't enroll.
  • S-corp owners who own more than 2% can qualify too, as long as the premiums are run through the S-corp and reported on their W-2.
  • Long-term care premiums can be included, up to age-based limits.

Some filers need IRS Form 7206 to calculate it. Your tax preparer can confirm how it applies to you.

When Can You Sign Up?

For Marketplace plans, open enrollment for 2027 coverage runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027. Enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1. Outside that window, you generally need a qualifying life event — like losing other coverage — to enroll.

Private underwritten plans often have more flexible start dates, which can help if you're between jobs or just starting out on your own.

Hiring Your First Employee? Your Options Change

Once you bring on a W-2 employee, group options open up — including level-funded plans and health reimbursement arrangements. Our guide on how small business owners get health insurance covers every option once you have a team.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do companies have to provide health insurance to 1099 contractors?

No. Independent contractors aren't employees, so the companies they work with aren't required to offer them health coverage. Contractors buy their own coverage through the Marketplace, a private plan, or a spouse's plan.

Can I deduct health insurance if I'm a 1099 contractor?

Usually, yes. If you have a net profit from self-employment, you can generally deduct premiums for yourself, your spouse, and your dependents through the self-employed health insurance deduction. You can't take it for months you were eligible for a subsidized employer plan, including through a spouse.

Did ACA subsidies change for 2026?

Yes. The enhanced premium tax credits expired at the start of 2026, so subsidies are smaller for many people, and households earning more than 400% of the federal poverty level generally no longer qualify for any tax credit.

When is open enrollment for 2027 health insurance?

On HealthCare.gov, open enrollment for 2027 coverage runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027. Enroll by December 15 for coverage that starts January 1.

The Bottom Line

Being self-employed means building your own benefits — but you have more options than most people realize, and the tax deduction takes a real bite out of the cost. With subsidies shrinking in 2026, it's worth comparing the Marketplace and private plans side by side before you renew.
